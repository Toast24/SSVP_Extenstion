We present an extended implementation of
the Synergistic Semantic-Visual Prompting
(SSVP) framework (1) for zero-shot industrial
anomaly detection, evaluated on the MVTec
AD cable category. Our implementation extends the original research code in three directions: (1) a captioning branch that produces
human-readable defect descriptions alongside
pixel-level anomaly maps; (2) INT8 dynamic
quantisation of the caption text transformer
for edge-feasible inference; and (3) systematic
robustness evaluation under heavy synthetic
imaging noise. Our primary checkpoint (run21)
achieves 97.42% image-AUROC and 95.34%
pixel-AUROC on clean test images, degrading
gracefully to 87.00% and 91.82% respectively
under synthetic noise. Compression and distillation experiments confirm that neither depthpruning nor differentiated-pruning of the segmentation backbone meets the quality acceptance threshold, validating run21 as the deployment configuration. All experiments are scoped
to a single MVTec AD category due to compute and memory constraints, which we discuss
explicitly.

Introduction

Industrial quality control demands anomaly detection systems that are simultaneously accurate,
explainable, and deployable on constrained hardware. The dominant unsupervised paradigm —
fitting a model to a distribution of normal samples
and flagging deviations — achieves strong benchmark performance but fails on two deploymentcritical axes: it cannot generalise to unseen product
categories without retraining, and it produces no
human-interpretable output beyond a heatmap.
Zero-shot anomaly detection (ZSAD) (3), enabled by large vision–language models (VLMs)
such as CLIP, resolves the first limitation by
grounding anomaly reasoning in natural language.

Related Work

CLIP-based ZSAD. WinCLIP (4) first demonstrated that CLIP’s contrastive representations
could support zero-shot anomaly segmentation
through compositional prompt ensembles and
multi-scale window features, achieving 91.8%
image-AUROC on MVTec-AD without fine-tuning.
AnomalyCLIP (5) improved generalisation by

individual attention heads in the image encoder,
gaining up to 4.9 pp in pixel-level detection. Wang
et al. (11) and Deng et al. (10) respectively addressed prompt selection and fine-grained alignment. AA-CLIP (9) explicitly reorients CLIP features toward anomaly-discriminative directions via
auxiliary adapters.
Vision foundation models. AnomalyVFM (12)
shows that purely visual foundation models match
VLMs in ZSAD when equipped with diverse
synthetic training data and LoRA-style adapters,
achieving 94.1% mean image-AUROC across nine
datasets. FineLIP (13) extends CLIP’s 77-token
text limit, enabling richer defect prompts.
Multimodal reasoning and compression.
Anomaly-OV (15) introduces the first anomaly reasoning benchmark and specialist MLLM with a
Look-Twice Feature Matching mechanism, establishing that general-purpose MLLMs cannot describe fine-grained defects. For compression, the
Bulk-to-Budget study (16) identifies widthwise
structured pruning and hidden-state distillation as
the most effective MLLM compression strategy;
LLM-Sieve (17) achieves 20–75% parameter reduction via genetic-algorithm-guided non-uniform
pruning with only 1–5% accuracy loss. StructuralGLIP (14) proposes a latent prompt knowledge
bank directly applicable to category-specific defect
description.

Vision-Conditioned Prompt Generator
(VCPG). To overcome the limitations of static text
prompts in zero-shot settings, VCPG dynamically
generates input-dependent prompts using crossmodal attention. Visual features extracted from the
image guide the construction of textual queries,
allowing the model to adaptively focus on likely
anomaly patterns. For example, instead of relying
on a fixed prompt like “defective cable,” the model
generates context-aware prompts aligned with
observed visual irregularities.
Visual-Text Anomaly Mapper (VTAM).
VTAM produces the final anomaly prediction by
aligning visual features with text embeddings. A
key challenge in anomaly detection is the mismatch
between global similarity scores (image-level) and
local evidence (pixel-level). VTAM addresses
this using a dual-gated calibration mechanism:
one gate regulates global anomaly confidence,
while the other emphasizes localized deviations.
This prevents dominant background regions from
suppressing small but critical defect signals,
leading to more accurate segmentation masks.
Together, these components enable SSVP to perform zero-shot anomaly segmentation by jointly
reasoning over semantic context and structural detail, achieving strong performance without taskspecific supervision.

The segmentation model is built upon the Synergistic Semantic-Visual Prompting (SSVP) framework, designed for zero-shot anomaly detection
using Vision-Language Models (VLMs). Unlike
conventional approaches that rely on a single visual
backbone, SSVP combines complementary visual
representations to balance global semantic understanding with fine-grained structural sensitivity.
The backbone integrates two key feature sources:
(i) CLIP, which provides strong semantic alignment
between images and text, and (ii) DINOv2, which
captures rich structural and texture-level details.
These are fused through three tightly coupled modules:
Hierarchical Semantic-Visual Synergy
(HSVS). HSVS performs deep multi-scale fusion
by injecting DINO-derived structural features

3.2

Data Preparation

The MVTec AD cable category was re-split
into a 70/20/10 train/test/validation partition with aligned ground-truth masks using
prepare_cable_split.py. The re-split enforces
strict anti-leakage between splits: validation
images are withheld from training entirely and
used only for threshold calibration and early
stopping. This departs from the standard MVTec
AD protocol (which uses the official test split for
evaluation directly) to enable validation-driven
model selection without contaminating the
reported test metrics.
Compute justification. Limiting experiments
to the cable category reduces training time to a feasible range on a single GPU. Cable is selected as a

representative MVTec AD category with multiple
defect subtypes (bent wire, cable swap, combined,
cut inner insulation, cut sheath, missing cable, missing wire, poke insulation), providing sufficient defect diversity to exercise all model components.

To simulate realistic imaging degradation, synthetic heavy noise is applied to the test split
via evaluate_noise_robustness.py. The same
trained checkpoint and thresholds from the clean
evaluation are reused unchanged — no noisespecific fine-tuning is performed. This isolates
the model’s native robustness and yields a clean-tonoisy delta for each metric.

Training Protocol

Training uses the SSVP default configuration
(configs/default.yaml) with a maximum of 15
epochs and early stopping monitored on validation image-AUROC. Checkpoints are saved at each
validation improvement; the best checkpoint by
validation metric is used for all downstream evaluation. Anomaly thresholds are calibrated on the
validation split post-training, not on the test split.

3.6

Compression and Distillation Pipeline

Two
backbone
compression
strategies
are evaluated under a gated workflow
(run_compression_gate_pipeline.py): depth
pruning and differentiated (non-uniform) head
pruning. Each strategy runs a 3-epoch preliminary evaluation; only candidates that satisfy a
maximum performance drop threshold (7% on
image-AUROC relative to run21) proceed to the
full 15-epoch training. Separately, a distillation
workflow (run_staged_distillation.py) trains
smaller models with gate checks against the run21
baseline.

Captioning Branch

To enhance interpretability, a captioning branch
is integrated downstream of the segmentation
pipeline. While the SSVP backbone produces pixellevel anomaly maps, it does not inherently provide
human-readable explanations. This branch bridges
that gap by converting detected anomalies into concise textual descriptions.
After inference, high-confidence anomaly regions identified by VTAM are spatially localized
and cropped from the input image. These cropped
regions are paired with structured prompts and
passed to a pre-trained text transformer (LLMbased captioning module). The prompts are designed to guide the model toward defect-specific
descriptions rather than generic outputs.
To make the system practical for deployment, the
captioning model is optimized using INT8 dynamic
quantisation applied to all linear layers. This significantly reduces inference latency and memory
footprint without requiring retraining of the segmentation backbone. As a result, the system can
generate captions alongside segmentation outputs
in near real-time.
Additionally, a domain-specific prompt finetuning stage is supported. This improves the specificity of generated captions (e.g., producing “cable
swap defect” instead of a generic description), but
introduces additional computational overhead.
This captioning integration aligns with the
project’s goal of combining accuracy, robustness,
and explainability: the model not only detects
anomalies but also provides interpretable descriptions suitable for industrial inspection workflows
and human-in-the-loop validation.

Experimental Setup

Dataset. MVTec AD cable category (2), re-split
70/20/10. Defect types: bent wire, cable swap,
combined, cut inner insulation, cut sheath, missing
cable, missing wire, poke insulation.
Metrics. Image-level: AUROC (I-AUROC),
F1-Max (I-F1), Average Precision (I-AP). Pixellevel: AUROC (P-AUROC), Per-Region Overlap
(P-PRO), Average Precision (P-AP). All metrics
reported on the held-out test split; noise robustness
metrics reported separately on the same split with
synthetic noise applied.
Baselines. Run21 (the primary recommended
checkpoint) is compared against: earlier runs
(run15, run16_seg) which lacked full noise evaluation; post-run21 architectural variants (runs 22–30)
exploring LoRA, entropy regularisation, noisy denoising autoencoder (DAE) training, and robust
patch augmentation; and compression gate candidates.
Hardware and runtime. All experiments run
on a single GPU. Training runs to 15 epochs with
early stopping; ablation and gate candidates are
limited to 3 epochs to bound wall-clock time. This
3-epoch limit for ablations constitutes a deliberate
scope reduction: it provides sufficient signal to gate
compression candidates but may underestimate the

Limitations

Synthetic noise only. Noise robustness is assessed
using synthetic perturbations rather than real sensor noise profiles. Industrial imaging noise (motion
blur, lens flare, uneven illumination, compression
artefacts) has a different spectral and spatial structure; the clean-to-noisy deltas reported here may
not transfer directly.
3-epoch ablation ceiling. All ablation and compression gate candidates are trained for 3 epochs.
Some variants — particularly DAE training and
noisy calibration — may benefit from longer training schedules that are infeasible within the evaluation budget.
